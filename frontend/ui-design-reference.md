# My Design Philosophy for Nova

Not futuristic.

Not cyberpunk.

Not SaaS.

Not Apple clone.

Not ChatGPT clone.

Not "AI startup."

It should feel like something **Massive**, **Timeless**, **Alive**, and **Intimate** simultaneously.

When someone opens Nova, I don't want them thinking

> "Nice AI app."

I want

> "I've never seen software feel like this."

## If this were my startup...

I would not open Figma for the first month.

I'd create a **Nova Design Bible**.

It would be a 150-200 page document defining everything:

* color philosophy and material language,
* typography hierarchy down to optical sizing,
* spacing system (perhaps an 8pt base with intentional exceptions),
* motion grammar,
* elevation rules,
* iconography,
* illustrations,
* empty states,
* loading states,
* sound design,
* haptic language,
* copywriting voice,
* accessibility,
* theming,
* even how success, curiosity, uncertainty, and urgency are *visualized*.

Because the companies people remember didn't just design screens.

They invented a **visual language**.

---

### One suggestion that I think would be genuinely worthwhile

Treat Nova like a **luxury automobile interior**, not a website.

When you sit inside a high-end Porsche, Bentley, or Lexus, nothing screams for attention. The stitching, the materials, the resistance of every knob, the lighting, and the proportions all quietly tell you that someone obsessed over every millimeter.

That's the feeling I'd chase.

Not "the coolest AI UI."

The interface people subconsciously trust because **every detail feels intentional**.
